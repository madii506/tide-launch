the support line

The hedge pays for the dip.

A reserve that holds a hedge is usually worth the most exactly when a coin needs it: crashes pump the short Nasdaq and VIX.

30%The support line sits 30% under the coin's highest price of the last 24 hours.
1 / 5Under the line, the reserve spends up to a fifth of that coin's share per buyback, at most once an hour.
burnedBought-back coins are burned on-chain. The supply only goes down.
publicEvery fill, swap, buyback and burn is a transaction on Solscan, listed on the coin and in the reserve.

A reserve slows a dump; it can't stop a big one. There is no floor and no promise. In this first version the reserve wallet signs each buyback from the operator console.

the fine print, on-chain

Rules nobody can bend.

01

The reserve, on-chain

What the reserve holds is written into your coin's metadata link at launch. Anyone can read it, nobody can edit it.

02

1% of every trade

The launchpad fee fills the reserve. It's set in Raydium LaunchLab, not by this website.

03

One support line

30% under the coin's 24h high, the same for every coin. Under it the reserve buys back, at most once an hour.

04

Bought back, burned

Coins the reserve buys are burned. Every coin: 1,000,000,000 tokens, no mint or freeze authority.

05

Half the LP burned

At graduation half the liquidity is burned for good, the other half keeps filling the reserve.

06

No creator fee, no cut

Nobody takes a share of trades besides the reserve and LaunchLab's own 0.25%.

under the hood

Where the reserve trades.

The deepest Solana pool for each asset, live. The reserve buys and sells through Jupiter, which routes across all of them.

faq

Questions, answered.

What is TIDE?

A launchpad on Raydium LaunchLab where every coin gets a reserve and a support line. 1% of each coin's trading fills its reserve with a hedge. When the coin falls 30% under its 24h high, the reserve buys it back and burns it.

Why hold a hedge instead of SOL?

Because a hedge tends to be worth the most when everything else is red. Crashes pump the short Nasdaq and VIX, and gold often holds. That's when coins dump and a reserve is needed.

Is the support line a floor?

No. A reserve slows a dump; it can't stop a big one. A coin can still go to zero.

Who signs the buybacks?

The reserve wallet, from the operator console on this site. In this first version a person presses the button when a coin is under its line. Every buyback and burn is public on Solscan.

What can a reserve hold?

Up to four of nine assets: gold (GLDx, PAXG, XAUt0), silver (SLVon), the short Nasdaq (SQQQ), VIX (UVXY), dollars (USDC, USDT) and euros (EURC). Or a ready mix: balanced, storm, steady or gold.

How much does it cost to launch?

Only Solana network fees and rent, about 0.03 SOL. A first buy is optional. TIDE takes nothing.

Can the reserve mix change later?

No. It's written into the coin's metadata link when you launch. Launch a new coin for a new mix.

Where do the prices come from?

Dexscreener for live prices, GeckoTerminal for history, and Raydium's launch index for the coins on TIDE.
